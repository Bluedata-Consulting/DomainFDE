4. Measuring lead time
4.1 The three figures, and which one is meant
Three different numbers are routinely called "lead time" inside Meridian. They are not interchangeable and confusing them is the single most common analytical error in procurement reporting.
**Contracted target.** The figure in the agreement, held on the supplier record. A property of the contract, not of any shipment.
**Actual lead time.** Days elapsed from purchase order date to actual arrival at Portland. A property of a specific purchase order. Where the phrase "lead time" is used without qualification in this handbook, in reporting, or in conversation, it means this figure.
**Estimated arrival.** The carrier's ETA at time of booking. Useful for planning, useless for performance measurement, and never the basis of a breach assessment.
4.2 Variance and breach
Variance is actual lead time minus contracted target.
A purchase order is **within tolerance** where variance falls inside the tolerance band in §3. A purchase order is **in breach** where variance exceeds the tolerance band, unless relieved under §9.
