Where a call-off is raised late, the purchase order record must be annotated accordingly. An
unannotated late arrival is assessed as a supplier breach by default.

9. Force majeure
9.1 Standard clause
A supplier is relieved of lead-time obligations where performance is prevented by an event
beyond their reasonable control, including natural disaster, civil unrest, government action,
crop failure attributable to extreme weather, and labour action at origin not involving the
supplier's own workforce.

Relief requires written notice within five business days of the supplier becoming aware of
the event, and requires the supplier to demonstrate that the event actually prevented
performance rather than merely made it more difficult.

9.2 Carve-out — port congestion and vessel scheduling
Applies to Finca La Esperanza (SUP-01) and Lintong Highlands (SUP-04) only.
The following are expressly excluded from force majeure relief under these two
agreements:
* port congestion at the load port
* vessel roll-over, missed sailings and carrier scheduling failures
* container availability and equipment shortage
* delays in export documentation or customs clearance at origin

The commercial rationale is that these events are foreseeable at both ports, are within the
supplier's capacity to manage through earlier booking and alternate carrier arrangements,
and have materialised repeatedly. The supplier is expected to book with sufficient margin
that ordinary congestion does not breach the contracted lead time.

A delay caused by port congestion or a missed sailing at Cartagena or Belawan is therefore
a breach, not a relieved event, and counts toward the review triggers in §11.

Suppliers under §9.2 were compensated for accepting the carve-out through a price
adjustment at the relevant renewal. It is a paid-for term, not an imposition, and should be
enforced accordingly.

9.3 Relief does not cancel the operational consequence
A supplier properly relieved under §9.1 still leaves Meridian without coffee. Force majeure
governs contractual liability, not production planning. A relieved delay must still be raised
operationally so that quality and production can plan a response under MR-QA-STD-002
§5, and so that the customer-facing consequence is attributed correctly under MR-CC-POL-
004 §7.1.
