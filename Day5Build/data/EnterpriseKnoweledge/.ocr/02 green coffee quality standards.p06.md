Where a substitute component genuinely requires a different approach, that is a new
product specification and goes through §3, not through an undocumented profile
adjustment on the day.

9. Traceability
Every finished bag must be traceable to its roast batch, every roast batch to its green lot,
every green lot to its purchase order, and every purchase order to its supplier. The chain is:
order line -> roast batch -> green lot -> purchase order -> supplier

This chain exists so that a quality complaint can be resolved against evidence rather than
against opinion. Where the chain is broken at any hop, the complaint cannot be attributed,
and under MR-CC-POL-004 §7.1 an unattributable quality complaint is treated as
company-attributable.

Traceability records are retained for three years.

10. Known risks
Recorded here because they have materialised before.

Single-origin dependency on the Colombian base. Cascadia Blend's base component has
one qualified supplier. Any interruption to that supply forces a choice between a
substitution above the §5.3 threshold and a stock-out. There is no third option, and
pretending otherwise under pressure is how §5.4 gets missed. Qualification of a second
Colombian source has been on the procurement plan since 2024.

Aged balance-component lots. Brazilian natural lots held for the balance position tend to
sit longest in the silo and are the most likely to be reached for in a shortage. They are also
the most likely to have aged past their recorded score. See §4 on re-cupping at nine months.

Substitution under despatch pressure. Substitution decisions are made on production
mornings when a roast is scheduled and the silo is short. That is precisely when the
authority requirements in §5.2 and the notification obligation in §5.4 are most likely to be
skipped, because both require someone outside the roastery to be available.
