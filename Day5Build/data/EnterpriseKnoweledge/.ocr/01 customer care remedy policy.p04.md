The cap applies per incident, not per contact. A customer who opens three tickets about the same defective delivery is one incident. A customer who has a delivery failure in January and a quality failure in March is two incidents and two caps.

6. Approval matrix
| Remedy value (USD) | Authority required |
| :----------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Up to 150          | Customer care agent, acting alone                                                 |
| 151 to 500         | Customer care supervisor                                                          |
| 501 to 1,250       | Director of Customer Experience, or CX supervisor with written Finance concurrence |
| Above 1,250        | Director of Customer Experience and Finance Director, jointly                     |

The 150 threshold is absolute. No role, system, automation or agent may issue a remedy above USD 150 without a named human approver recorded against the case. This applies irrespective of customer tier, irrespective of how obviously justified the remedy is, and irrespective of whether the value sits inside the tier cap. A gold-tier customer owed the full 120 cap is within an agent's authority; the same customer owed 160 under an uplift is not.
Automated systems that propose remedies must record them in a proposed state and must not represent a proposal to the customer as a decision.

7. Conditions and exceptions
This section did not exist in v3.1 and is the substantive change in v4.0. Under v3.1, remedy value was determined solely by customer tier. Post-incident review through 2024 and 2025 found this produced perverse outcomes: a customer harmed by a Meridian production failure received the same ceiling as a customer inconvenienced by a carrier, despite the two cases differing entirely in attribution and in relationship damage.

7.1 Attribution categories
Every remedy decision must first assign one of four attribution categories.
Customer-attributable. Incorrect address, refused delivery, storage or brewing error, change of mind. No remedy beyond Step 1 is owed, although discretionary Step 2 remains available for relationship reasons.
Third-party-attributable. Carrier delay, carrier damage, weather event, port closure, public-holiday cut-off, or any failure originating outside Meridian's control. Standard caps in §5 apply unchanged.
