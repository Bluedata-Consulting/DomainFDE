No customer notification was issued. Quality complaints began arriving on 25 November,
roughly two weeks after the first affected batches shipped, and continued into mid-
December.
Timeline
• 14 Oct — Belawan port closure begins
• 18 Oct — Supplier notifies delay; relief claimed under force majeure
• 12 Nov — First substituted roast batch produced. QA note recorded. Substitution
authorised verbally by the roast lead
• 27 Nov — Colombian-sourced Sumatran replacement arrives; substitution ends
• 25 Nov — First quality complaint received
• 2 Dec — Complaint volume recognised as a cluster rather than noise
• 3 Dec — Root cause established by tracing complaints to batch records
Root cause
A blend base component was substituted with a materially lower-scoring lot, and
customers were not told.
Contributing factors
Five, and all five are the reason this post-mortem matters.
No notification obligation existed. The QA manual at v2.2 did not require customer
notification for any substitution. The decision to notify was discretionary and nobody made
it.
Authority was inadequate. The substitution was authorised verbally by the roast lead. The
manual at the time permitted this. A base-component substitution on a flagship blend was
being decided by the person under the most schedule pressure.
Score delta was calculated against the wrong baseline. The team compared the substitute
against the specification minimum (0.8, felt tolerable) rather than against the lot actually in
use (3.9, obviously not tolerable). Customers do not taste the specification. They taste last
month's bag.
The complaint cluster took seven days to recognise. Individual agents saw individual
complaints. Nobody was looking at complaint rate by product against a baseline.
The connection to the port closure was never made until day 27. Procurement knew
about the delay in October. Customer care saw complaints in late November. The two facts
sat in different systems owned by different teams and nobody joined them, because no
single role had reason to look at both.
Customer impact
