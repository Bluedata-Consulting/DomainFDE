Single-origin products may not be substituted at all. If the named origin is unavailable, the product goes out of stock. This is not negotiable and has been tested twice.
4. Lot release
A green lot may enter production only when all of the following exist on the lot record:
* a cupping score from three assessors
* a moisture reading between 10.0 and 12.0 per cent
* a defect count within grade
* an intake date and a linked purchase order
* no open supplier claim against the lot
A lot that has been in the silo more than nine months from intake must be re-cupped before use. Green coffee ages. A lot that scored 84 on arrival may not score 84 a year later, and an aged lot used at its original score is a specification failure hiding behind a stale record.
5. Substitution policy
This is the most consequential section in this manual and the one most often applied under time pressure.
5.1 When substitution may be considered
Substitution of a blend component may be considered only when:
* the specified component is genuinely unavailable, not merely inconvenient to retrieve, and
* the alternative component meets the minimum score for the position it is filling, and
* the substitution is recorded on the batch record before the roast, not after
Production pressure is not a justification. Running out of a product is an acceptable outcome and is always preferable to shipping something the customer did not order under a name they recognise.
5.2 Authority

| Substitution | Authority |
|---|---|
| Within the same origin, score delta 1.0 or less | Roast lead |
| Cross-origin, score delta 2.0 or less | QA Lead |
| Cross-origin, score delta above 2.0 | QA Lead and Head of Quality, jointly |
| Any substitution affecting a blend's named base component | Head of Quality, in writing |
| Any substitution on a single-origin product | Not permitted under any authority |
